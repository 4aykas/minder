## 1. Purpose of the session

The session is a brainstorm between two management teams, not a pitch and not a
due diligence meeting. The goal is a shared visual map that shows:

- what each side already has
- what each side wants from the other
- where the real points of contact are
- what a joint operating model can look like

The output must be readable by everyone in the room in under a minute, and deep
enough to carry a two-hour discussion.

## 2. Framing principles

These govern every design and wording decision in the artifact.

1. **A network of equals, not an acquisition.** Haskoning is a Dutch group with
   subsidiaries in several countries. TEBIN is one more node. Every exchange
   arrow points both ways.
2. **Symmetry is the message.** "What we give" and "what we want" get equal
   visual weight. A one-sided board changes the tone of the room.
3. **Name the flow, not the concept.** No arrow is labelled "synergy". Arrows
   are labelled "certified Polish specialists", "access to Swiss contractors",
   "MEP discipline leads", "as-built scanning".
4. **Autonomy is shown, not written.** TEBIN keeps its own contour as a network
   node with its own boundary. No sentence claims independence. The structure
   says it.
5. **Honest gaps build credit.** Open questions — security, data location,
   licence overlap, who owns standards — are on the board because TEBIN raised
   them, not because someone asked.
6. **Consistency with the DD package.** Nothing on the board contradicts the
   wording already submitted in the due diligence response. Divergence gets
   noticed.

## 4. What moves across the network

Five flow types. Each gets its own colour in the artifact.

1. **Leads** — project opportunities shared in both directions.
2. **People and competencies** — engineering capacity, discipline leads,
   project managers.
3. **Standards and certification** — local signing authority, methodology,
   quality frameworks.
4. **Local engineering experience** — market knowledge, norms, client relations.
5. **Training and know-how** — learning material, mentoring, process maturity.

## 6. What TEBIN wants from Haskoning

- **Local legitimacy in Poland.** TEBIN has a Polish entity but almost no
  involvement in Polish projects. The ask is joint entry into Polish work
  together with Haskoning's Polish subsidiary or its partners. TEBIN delivers,
  the local side carries the brand and the signature. This is the single most
  concrete business proposition on the board.
- **Training material and methodology.** Haskoning's learning resources are
  strong and TEBIN wants access to them.
- **Process maturity.** Established corporate processes worth learning.
- **Mentoring.** Senior Haskoning people who can train TEBIN teams.
- **Shared leads** across the group's subsidiaries.

## 7. The local certification model

This is a structural fact that applies across every market and explains why the
network works.

TEBIN holds formal signing authority only in Ukraine. In Germany, Poland,
Switzerland and elsewhere, TEBIN's engineers are qualified but not locally
certified, so drawings are signed by the local party. TEBIN prepares the work;
the local entity carries the responsibility.

On the board this is one horizontal line running through all markets, not a
footnote per country. It is not a weakness. It is exactly the gap that a group
with local subsidiaries closes, and it makes the exchange model self-evident.

The mirror case is Ukraine, where TEBIN holds the local legitimacy and the
partner brings scale and methodology.

## 9. People

Three separate dimensions. They are often merged and should not be.

**Competencies** — disciplines, seniority, languages. The competency matrix is
the existing source. Roles and counts only. No individual names while due
diligence is open.

**Development** — the inbound flow: learning material, methodology, mentoring
from Haskoning's senior people.

**Mobility and certification** — who can work formally in which market. This
determines which leads can actually be taken, and it is the most valuable
people-related fact in the network.

**Language.** English across project staff, with room to improve. No native
Polish speakers. TEBIN needs Polish ambassadors and plans to hire in Poland
within a year.

**Model of cooperation.** Not rotation of staff between offices. Exchange of
experience, training, and full-time allocation of TEBIN people to projects —
proper project integration rather than fragmented involvement.

**Ownership.** Max and Ania hold the relationship on the TEBIN side.

## 10. Delivery models

- **Embedded in the client team.** Several German cases where TEBIN works as
  part of the client's own team. This is close to how group subsidiaries
  already operate together.
- **Discipline ownership.** The direction TEBIN wants to move toward: full
  responsibility for a discipline package, which depends on the local
  certification question above.

## 11. IT, security and infrastructure

Presented as fact. The maturity speaks for itself and removes most security
questions before they are asked.

- No local perimeter. Microsoft 365 and Azure.
- Cloudflare at the edge. Zero Trust access: identity, not network location.
- Tailscale as a mesh option for distributed teams `[under consideration]`.
- ISO 27001 in preparation.

Tone: Zero Trust is the common language, not a proposal. Haskoning has its own
access standards. TEBIN's position is that it works in a zero trust model and
adapts the tooling. Nothing is pushed as better.

## 12. Digital roadmap

- ISO 9001 quality control.

This is the bridge between TEBIN's short adoption cycle and Haskoning's
corporate maturity.

## 13. Not for the board

- Rates, margins, and any pricing.
- Headcount economics.
- Individual names while due diligence is open.
- Any claim of independence stated in words.
- Any statement that diverges from the DD package wording.
- The Ukrainian war context as a narrative. It stays as structural fact: two
  legal entities, engineering base in Ukraine, active Ukrainian projects.
